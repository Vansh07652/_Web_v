# Phosphodiesterase 5 Inhibitors

> **Book:** [Pharmacology for Nurses](../../README.md)  
> **Chapter:** [Chapter 36: Reproductive Health Drugs](../README.md)  
> **Topic:** 7  
> **Source structure:** OpenStax public textbook outline

## Overview

**Phosphodiesterase 5 (PDE5) inhibitors** are a drug class best known for treating erectile dysfunction (ED) and also used for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). The class includes sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, and avanafil. Their mechanism is a direct application of the erection physiology reviewed in Topic 5: sexual stimulation releases nitric oxide, which generates **cGMP**, the messenger that relaxes the smooth muscle of the penile erectile tissue. The enzyme PDE5 normally breaks cGMP down; these drugs inhibit PDE5, so cGMP lasts longer and the erectile response is stronger. Crucially, the drugs do not create erections on their own — they enhance a response that sexual stimulation has already started.

The same enzyme exists in pulmonary vascular smooth muscle, which is why these drugs are also used to relax pulmonary vessels in PAH. The class has one headline safety rule that every nurse must internalize: PDE5 inhibitors must not be combined with nitrates, because the two drug families together can cause life-threatening hypotension.

## Why This Matters

- **Extremely common therapy.** Erectile dysfunction affects a large share of men, and PDE5 inhibitors are among the most prescribed drugs in the world. Nurses will meet them constantly — often as "as-needed" medications that patients forget to mention.
- **The nitrate interaction is potentially lethal.** Nitroglycerin and related nitrates are used for angina. A patient taking both a PDE5 inhibitor and a nitrate can develop profound hypotension. Medication reconciliation that catches this combination is a genuine life-saving nursing act.
- **Education prevents complications.** Teaching patients about priapism (prolonged erection that requires emergency care) and about realistic expectations (stimulation is required) prevents harm and disappointment.
- **Beyond ED.** The PAH indication shows the same mechanism applied to a different vascular bed — a classic "one mechanism, two diseases" exam theme.
- **Exam relevance:** mechanism (NO → cGMP, PDE5 inhibition), the nitrate contraindication, CYP3A4 interactions, and adverse-effect profiles are high-yield test items.

## Core Concepts

### The physiology of erection: NO → cGMP → relaxation

Erection is a vascular event. Sexual stimulation activates the parasympathetic system, which triggers release of **nitric oxide (NO)** from the endothelium of the corpora cavernosa. NO diffuses into the smooth muscle cells and activates **guanylate cyclase**, which produces **cGMP**. cGMP relaxes the smooth muscle, the erectile tissue (corpora cavernosa) fills with blood, veins are compressed, and the penis becomes rigid. The signal is terminated by **phosphodiesterase 5**, the enzyme that degrades cGMP; when cGMP falls, the smooth muscle contracts again and the tissue returns to flaccidity. This single pathway explains the entire drug class.

### What PDE5 inhibitors do

PDE5 inhibitors block the enzyme that destroys cGMP. With PDE5 inhibited, cGMP accumulates during sexual stimulation, smooth muscle relaxation is stronger and longer-lasting, and erection is enhanced. Three consequences follow logically:

- **They require sexual stimulation.** No NO, no cGMP to protect — so these drugs do not cause spontaneous erections and are not "aphrodisiacs"; they do not increase libido.
- **They affect other smooth muscle too.** PDE5 exists elsewhere, which explains class-typical adverse effects: headache, facial flushing, nasal congestion, and dyspepsia.
- **Members differ in practical ways.** Onset, duration, effect of food, and metabolism vary among the class members (for example, some act much longer than others, and food can alter absorption of some). Product-specific differences must be verified against current labeling — never assumed.

### Beyond ED: pulmonary arterial hypertension

PDE5 is also present in pulmonary vascular smooth muscle. Inhibiting it there relaxes pulmonary vessels, lowering pulmonary vascular resistance — the rationale for using some PDE5 inhibitors to treat PAH. The same drug, a different indication, and typically a different dosing regimen: this is a reminder that indication determines how a drug is used, and that prescribing is always indication-specific and must be verified.

### The nitrate contraindication

Nitrates — nitroglycerin in its various forms (sublingual tablets, sprays, patches, pastes) and isosorbide products — work by donating NO, which raises cGMP. Combining a nitrate with a PDE5 inhibitor means two mechanisms pushing cGMP up at once: vasodilation becomes profound and hypotension can be life-threatening. This is why the combination is contraindicated in current labeling, and why the nurse must ask about **"as-needed" chest pain medications** during every medication review — patients often do not volunteer them. When a patient with chest pain who has taken a PDE5 inhibitor needs emergency care, the health care team must be told about the PDE5 inhibitor so treatment decisions can account for it.

### Adverse effects and drug interactions

Class-typical effects are extensions of vasodilation: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, and dizziness. **Visual changes** (a blue tinge to vision) can occur with sildenafil because it also inhibits PDE6 in the retina; the effect is transient, but any vision change should be reported. **Priapism** — an erection lasting more than about four hours — is a rare but urgent adverse event because prolonged erection can damage erectile tissue; patients must know to seek emergency care immediately. Rare events such as sudden hearing loss have been reported and warrant flagging for review rather than dismissal.

Drug interactions beyond nitrates deserve attention: **CYP3A4 inhibitors** (some azole antifungals, some macrolides, and grapefruit juice) can raise PDE5 inhibitor levels, and combining PDE5 inhibitors with alpha blockers (Topic 8) requires caution per current guidance. Always verify the full medication list and current labeling.

### Nursing considerations

The nursing role spans medication reconciliation (nitrates!), cardiac risk awareness, teaching, and respectful communication about sexual health:

- Ask about all erectile dysfunction products — prescribed or obtained otherwise — during every admission and medication review.
- Confirm nitrate use in any form before a PDE5 inhibitor is considered, and escalate concerns to the prescriber and pharmacist.
- Teach realistic expectations (stimulation required, not an aphrodisiac) and the priapism emergency rule.
- Document sexual-health conversations with person-first, nonjudgmental language; scope and documentation requirements vary by institution.

## ELI-10: Explain Like I'm 10

Inside the penis, a chemical message makes the blood vessels relax and fill with blood, which causes an erection. An enzyme is like a cleanup crew that quickly removes that message. PDE5 inhibitors tell the cleanup crew to take a break, so the message stays around longer and the erection is stronger — but only if the brain already sent the message. These medicines do not work without that message, and they must never be mixed with heart medicines called nitrates, because together they can make blood pressure drop dangerously low.

## High-Yield Points

- **Mechanism:** PDE5 inhibitors block the enzyme that degrades cGMP, enhancing NO-driven smooth muscle relaxation — they require sexual stimulation and do not raise libido.
- **The nitrate rule:** combining PDE5 inhibitors with nitrates (any form) can cause life-threatening hypotension — a contraindication to verify in current labeling.
- **Ask about "as-needed" drugs:** sublingual nitroglycerin for chest pain is easy to miss in medication reconciliation.
- **Same mechanism, second indication:** pulmonary vasodilation makes some PDE5 inhibitors useful in PAH, with a different regimen.
- **Class-typical effects** are vasodilation effects: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion.
- **Priapism teaching:** prolonged erection is an emergency — seek care immediately to prevent tissue damage.
- **Interactions:** CYP3A4 inhibitors (some antifungals, some macrolides, grapefruit juice) can raise drug levels; alpha-blocker combinations need caution per current guidance.
- **Verify, never assume:** onset, duration, food effects, and dosing differ among class members — check current labeling, the formulary, and prescriber orders.

## Key Terms

| Term | Plain-language definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| PDE5 | Phosphodiesterase 5: the enzyme that degrades cGMP | The molecular target of the entire drug class |
| cGMP | Second messenger that relaxes cavernosal smooth muscle | The molecule the drugs protect so erections strengthen |
| Nitric oxide (NO) | Endothelial messenger that triggers cGMP production | The start of the erection pathway and the mechanism nitrates mimic |
| Corpus cavernosum | Erectile tissue that fills with blood during erection | Where the NO → cGMP → relaxation sequence happens |
| Priapism | Prolonged, painful erection lasting beyond normal | Rare emergency; delayed care can damage erectile tissue |
| Nitrate | Drug class (e.g., nitroglycerin) that raises NO/cGMP | Absolute contraindication with PDE5 inhibitors |
| PAH | Pulmonary arterial hypertension: high pressure in lung arteries | Second indication for some PDE5 inhibitors |
| CYP3A4 | Liver enzyme that metabolizes many PDE5 inhibitors | Inhibitors of it can raise drug levels → interaction risk |

## Example: The Medication Review That Caught a Dangerous Combination

Mr. F., age 62, is admitted for chest pain. During admission, the nurse asks about all medications — prescribed, over-the-counter, and "as needed." Mr. F. mentions a sublingual nitroglycerin spray he keeps in his pocket, and then, hesitantly, a PDE5 inhibitor he obtained online last month.

The nurse's reasoning follows the mechanism:

1. **Recognize the risk.** The nitroglycerin (a nitrate) donates NO; the PDE5 inhibitor blocks cGMP breakdown. Together, two mechanisms raise cGMP at once → profound vasodilation → dangerous hypotension.
2. **Verify.** The nurse checks current labeling and the facility formulary for the combination, confirms the contraindication, and consults the pharmacist.
3. **Act.** The nurse documents both drugs, flags the interaction for the prescriber, and teaches Mr. F. why the combination is unsafe — including the instruction to tell emergency personnel about the PDE5 inhibitor if he ever needs care for chest pain.
4. **Teach the class.** The nurse also explains that PDE5 inhibitors require sexual stimulation, do not increase desire, and that a prolonged erection is an emergency — so Mr. F. knows the whole picture, not just the warning.

> **Safety note:** This scenario illustrates mechanisms and nursing reasoning only. All prescribing decisions, contraindication management, and emergency treatment belong to the prescriber and current evidence — always verify against current references, the facility formulary, and the prescriber's orders.

## Common Confusions

| Do not confuse | With | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| PDE5 inhibitors | Nitrates | Both raise cGMP-related vasodilation, but nitrates donate NO while PDE5 inhibitors block cGMP breakdown — combining them is dangerous |
| "PDE5 inhibitors cause erections" | They enhance existing responses | They only work with sexual stimulation; they do not cause spontaneous erections and are not aphrodisiacs |
| Sildenafil's vision changes | A serious eye emergency | Transient blue-tinged vision comes from PDE6 inhibition in the retina — report it, but it is typically harmless and transient |
| Different PDE5 inhibitors | Identical drugs | Onset, duration, and food effects differ among members — verify product-specific facts |
| ED treatment | Treatment of low libido | PDE5 inhibitors address erectile function, not desire |
| Rare reported events (e.g., hearing loss) | Common effects | They are rare and warrant flagging for review, not routine counseling as common effects |
