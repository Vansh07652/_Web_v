# Epithelial Tissue

> **Book:** [Anatomy and Physiology 2e](../../README.md)  
> **Chapter:** [Chapter 4: The Tissue Level of Organization](../README.md)  
> **Topic:** 2  
> **Source structure:** OpenStax public textbook outline

## Overview

**Epithelial tissue** is the body's covering and lining tissue. It forms the outer layer of the skin, lines the mouth, stomach, airways, and blood vessels, and makes up the glands that secrete sweat, mucus, hormones, and digestive juices. Epithelial cells pack tightly together with little material between them and sit on a thin **basement membrane** anchoring them to the connective tissue beneath. Epithelium is named by a two-part system: the number of **layers** (simple, stratified, or pseudostratified) and the **shape** of the surface cells (squamous, cuboidal, columnar, or transitional). The classification predicts function: thin, one-layered epithelia allow exchange; thick, multi-layered epithelia protect.

## Why This Matters

Epithelium is the body's first line of defense and its gateway for exchange. The one-cell-thick lining of the lung alveoli lets oxygen enter the blood in a fraction of a second; the stratified epithelium of the skin keeps water in and microbes out; the ciliated airway epithelium sweeps dust and germs up and out. Because epithelium regenerates so readily, cuts and mouth sores heal quickly — and because so much of the body is lined by it, most human cancers are **carcinomas** arising from epithelium (a commonly taught fact). A biopsy report naming the epithelial type tells you where the sample came from and whether it looks normal.

## Core Concepts

### Hallmarks of Epithelial Tissue

Epithelium is defined by five features:

- **Cellularity and contacts:** almost entirely cells packed closely together, with minimal extracellular material, joined by tight junctions (sealing), desmosomes (anchoring), and gap junctions (communication).
- **Polarity and attachment:** every cell has an **apical** surface (free, facing the lumen or exterior) and a **basal** surface resting on the **basement membrane**, a thin protein layer anchoring the tissue to the connective tissue beneath; the two surfaces differ — microvilli, for example, are apical.
- **Avascularity with high regeneration:** no blood vessels of its own — nutrients diffuse in from below — yet cells divide frequently to replace those lost at the surface.

### Classification by Cell Shape

- **Squamous** cells are flat and scale-like — ideal for rapid diffusion and filtration (alveoli, capillary walls).
- **Cuboidal** cells are about as tall as wide, like dice — suited to secretion and absorption (kidney tubules, gland ducts).
- **Columnar** cells are tall and narrow — specialized for secretion and absorption, often with microvilli or cilia (gut lining, uterine tubes).
- **Transitional** cells change shape — cuboidal when relaxed, flattened when stretched — and occur only in the urinary system (bladder, ureters, urethra), allowing organs to expand.

### Classification by Layers

- **Simple epithelium** is one layer, every cell touching the basement membrane; it suits diffusion, filtration, absorption, and secretion — but offers little protection.
- **Stratified epithelium** is two or more layers thick; only the deepest cells touch the basement membrane. It protects against abrasion where surfaces take a beating (skin, mouth, esophagus) and is named by the shape of its **apical (topmost) layer**, not the basal layer.
- **Pseudostratified epithelium** looks layered because nuclei sit at different heights, but it is one layer — every cell touches the basement membrane. It is usually columnar and often ciliated, as in the airways.

### The Named Epithelia and Where They Live

- **Simple squamous:** lung alveoli, blood vessel linings (endothelium), serous membranes, kidney glomeruli.
- **Simple cuboidal:** kidney tubules, small gland ducts, ovary surfaces.
- **Simple columnar:** the gut lining (microvilli, goblet cells) and the ciliated uterine tubes.
- **Pseudostratified columnar:** the respiratory airways, where cilia and mucus work as an escalator.
- **Stratified squamous:** the skin (keratinized, waterproof) and the mouth, esophagus, and vagina (non-keratinized, moist).
- **Stratified cuboidal and columnar:** rare — the larger ducts of some glands.
- **Transitional:** urinary bladder, ureters, and urethra — stretches without tearing.

### Glandular Epithelium

Glands form when epithelial cells grow downward into the connective tissue. **Exocrine glands** keep a duct and release their products onto a surface — sweat and saliva are examples. **Endocrine glands** lose their ducts and release hormones directly into the blood (thyroid, adrenal). Glands may be unicellular — a mucus-secreting **goblet cell** is one — or multicellular (salivary glands).

### Surface Specializations

- **Microvilli:** finger-like apical folds that increase surface area for absorption — the "brush border" of the small intestine and kidney; they do not move.
- **Cilia:** longer, motile hair-like extensions that beat in waves to move fluid or particles — mucus up the airways, an egg along the uterine tube.
- **Keratin:** a tough protein deposited in the apical cells of skin epithelium, making the surface waterproof and abrasion-resistant.

## ELI-10: Explain Like I'm 10

Imagine wall tiles. A single layer of thin tiles lets things pass through quickly — simple squamous epithelium, like the air sacs of the lungs. Stacked thick tiles protect what is behind them — stratified epithelium, like your skin. And some tiles have tiny brushes — cilia — that sweep dust and mucus from your airways.

## High-Yield Points

- Epithelium is classified by layers (simple, stratified, pseudostratified) × apical cell shape (squamous, cuboidal, columnar, transitional).
- Epithelium is avascular — nutrients diffuse from connective tissue across the basement membrane.
- Simple = exchange/absorption/secretion; stratified = protection; pseudostratified = one layer that looks layered.
- Name stratified epithelium by its apical layer — skin is "stratified squamous."
- Transitional epithelium occurs only in the urinary system and stretches.
- Epithelial cells regenerate rapidly — why skin and mucous membranes heal quickly.
- Most human cancers are carcinomas — cancers of epithelium (commonly taught).
- Goblet cells are unicellular exocrine glands; microvilli absorb, cilia move materials.

## Key Terms

| Term | Plain-language definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Epithelial tissue | The covering and lining tissue of the body; also forms glands | The body's barrier, exchange surface, and secretion machinery |
| Apical / basal surfaces | The free surface facing the lumen or exterior / the attached surface facing the basement membrane | Each surface has distinct specializations and roles |
| Basement membrane | Thin protein layer separating epithelium from connective tissue | Anchors epithelium and supports regeneration |
| Simple / stratified / pseudostratified | One layer / multiple layers / one layer that looks layered | Predicts exchange versus protection |
| Squamous / cuboidal / columnar | Flat / cube-shaped / tall cell shapes | Each shape suits a function: diffusion, secretion, or absorption |
| Transitional | Shape-changing epithelium of the urinary system | Allows the bladder and ureters to stretch |
| Microvilli | Non-motile apical folds that increase surface area | Boost absorption in the gut and kidney |
| Cilia | Motile apical hairs that move fluid or particles | The mucus escalator of the airways |
| Goblet cell | A unicellular mucus-secreting gland | Protects and lubricates gut and airway surfaces |
| Exocrine / endocrine gland | Ducted gland secreting to a surface / ductless gland secreting into blood | Explains how secretions reach their targets |

## Example: A Breath, a Swallow, and a Scraped Knee

Follow one breath of air down the trachea, whose lining is pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium: goblet cells trap dust in mucus, and cilia beat in waves, sweeping the mucus upward to be swallowed — the "mucociliary escalator." The air reaches the alveoli, whose walls are simple squamous epithelium one cell thick; the barrier is so thin that oxygen diffuses across in under a second — a perfect match of structure and function.

Swallowing is different: the esophagus is lined with non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, built for abrasion. And a scraped knee heals because basal cells of the skin's stratified squamous epithelium divide rapidly, pushing new cells outward. One classification, three jobs: pseudostratified ciliated columnar moves debris, simple squamous exchanges gas, stratified squamous protects — the name is a shortcut to the function.

## Common Confusions

| Do not confuse | With | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudostratified | Stratified | Pseudostratified is one layer — every cell touches the basement membrane; only basal cells do in stratified tissue |
| Simple squamous | Stratified squamous | Simple squamous is one flat layer for diffusion (alveoli, vessels); stratified squamous is many layers for protection (skin, mouth, esophagus) |
| Microvilli | Cilia | Microvilli are non-motile folds that increase surface area; cilia are motile and move materials |
| Exocrine gland | Endocrine gland | Exocrine secretes through ducts to a surface; endocrine secretes hormones into the blood |
| Endothelium | Epithelium in general | Endothelium is the simple squamous lining of blood vessels and the heart |
| "Stratified tissue is named by its basal layer" | Naming rule | Stratified tissue is named by its apical (topmost) layer |
