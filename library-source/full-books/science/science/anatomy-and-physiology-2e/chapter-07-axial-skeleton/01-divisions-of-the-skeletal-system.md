# Divisions of the Skeletal System

> **Book:** [Anatomy and Physiology 2e](../../README.md)  
> **Chapter:** [Chapter 7: Axial Skeleton](../README.md)  
> **Topic:** 1  
> **Source structure:** OpenStax public textbook outline

## Overview

The human skeleton is commonly described as having **206 bones** in the adult, and anatomists sort those bones into two main divisions based on where they sit in the body. The **axial skeleton** forms the central axis, or "trunk line," of the body: the skull, the auditory ossicles, the hyoid bone, the vertebral column, and the thoracic cage. The **appendicular skeleton** forms the appendages — the arms and legs — plus the two girdles (pectoral and pelvic) that connect the limbs to the axial skeleton. The word "axial" comes from *axis*: these are the bones arranged along the body's midline that support the head, neck, and trunk. The word "appendicular" comes from *appendage*: these are the bones of and near the limbs. Think of the axial skeleton as the body's central supporting pole and the appendicular skeleton as everything that hangs off it to move the body through the world.

## Why This Matters

Before you can name a single bone, you need to know which "team" it plays for. The axial skeleton protects the most vulnerable organs in the body — the brain, spinal cord, heart, and lungs — so injuries to axial bones carry different risks than injuries to limb bones. A rib fracture and a finger fracture may both be "broken bones," but the rib fracture threatens the lung underneath. In imaging, a chest X-ray and a head CT are essentially axial-skeleton studies, while a wrist X-ray is an appendicular study. For nursing and allied-health students, understanding the divisions explains why patients with spinal injuries are immobilized, why newborns are handled with head support, and why the sternum and vertebrae are common sites for bone marrow procedures. This topic is also the sorting key for every bone name you will meet in the rest of the book.

## Core Concepts

### The axial skeleton: the body's central axis

The axial skeleton contains about **80 bones** in the typical adult, in five groups:

- **Skull** (22 bones): 8 cranial bones that enclose the brain plus 14 facial bones.
- **Auditory ossicles** (6 bones): the malleus, incus, and stapes in each middle ear — technically part of the axial skeleton even though they are tiny.
- **Hyoid bone** (1 bone): a U-shaped bone in the anterior neck that supports the tongue; it is unusual because it does not directly articulate with any other bone (commonly taught as the only such bone in the body).
- **Vertebral column** (26 bones): 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, and 5 lumbar vertebrae, plus the sacrum (5 fused vertebrae) and the coccyx (3–4 fused vertebrae).
- **Thoracic cage** (25 bones): the sternum plus 12 pairs of ribs.

### The appendicular skeleton: limbs and girdles

The appendicular skeleton contains about **126 bones** in the typical adult:

- **Pectoral girdle** (4 bones): the clavicles and scapulae, which attach the upper limbs to the sternum and rib cage.
- **Upper limbs** (60 bones): humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, and 14 phalanges on each side (30 per limb).
- **Pelvic girdle** (2 bones): the two coxal (hip) bones, which attach the lower limbs to the sacrum.
- **Lower limbs** (60 bones): femur, tibia, fibula, patella, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, and 14 phalanges on each side (30 per limb).

### Where the two divisions meet

The divisions are not separate: the limbs must anchor to the axis to work. The upper limb hangs from the pectoral girdle, which reaches the axial skeleton at the **sternoclavicular joint** (the only bony joint between the upper limb and the trunk). The lower limb attaches through the pelvic girdle, whose coxal bones meet the sacrum at the **sacroiliac joints**. So every push, pull, or step transmits force from an appendicular bone, through a girdle, into the axial skeleton.

### Why the count is "about" 206

The 206-bone figure is a useful average, not a law of nature. Individual skeletons vary: **sesamoid bones** (small bones embedded in tendons, like the patella) develop in some people but not others; **sutural (Wormian) bones** form unpredictably inside skull sutures; and fusion patterns differ (the coccyx can have 3–5 pieces). A newborn's skeleton is commonly cited as having roughly **270 bones** because many bones that will later fuse (sacrum, coccyx, ilium–ischium–pubis, skull plates) are still separate at birth. Bone counts are therefore best described as typical rather than fixed.

### What the axial division actually does

The axial skeleton's jobs overlap with the skeleton as a whole, but its most distinctive role is **protection**: the skull shields the brain, the vertebral column shields the spinal cord, and the thoracic cage shields the heart, lungs, and great vessels. It also supports the body's upright posture, provides attachment surfaces for muscles of the head, neck, and trunk, houses blood-forming red marrow (especially in the sternum, vertebrae, and ribs), and serves — with the rest of the skeleton — as the body's main reservoir of calcium and phosphate.

## ELI-10: Explain Like I'm 10

Imagine your body is a tent. The **axial skeleton** is the center pole: the helmet-like skull on top, the stack of building blocks (backbone) in the middle, and the rib cage like curved hoops around your chest. The **appendicular skeleton** is everything attached to the pole so you can move: your arms, legs, and the straps (girdles) that hook them on. If the center pole breaks, the tent collapses; if a strap breaks, you just lose an arm.

## High-Yield Points

- Typical adult total: **206 bones** = **80 axial** + **126 appendicular**.
- Axial groups: skull (22), ossicles (6), hyoid (1), vertebral column (26), thoracic cage (25).
- Appendicular groups: pectoral girdle (4), upper limbs (60), pelvic girdle (2), lower limbs (60).
- The axial skeleton protects the brain, spinal cord, heart, and lungs — the "vital organs" of the trunk.
- The girdles are the mechanical link between limbs and axis: sternoclavicular joint above, sacroiliac joints below.
- The 206 count varies: sesamoid and sutural bones are unpredictable; the coccyx can fuse from 3–5 pieces.
- Newborns have more bones (commonly cited ~270) because many later fuse; the count falls as fusion proceeds.
- The hyoid bone is unique: it articulates with no other bone (commonly taught).
- On exams, "which structure is NOT axial?" — the classic answer is a girdle bone such as the scapula or clavicle.

## Key Terms

| Term | Plain-Language Definition | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Axial skeleton | The bones along the body's central axis: skull, ossicles, hyoid, vertebral column, thoracic cage | Protects the brain, spinal cord, heart, and lungs; supports posture |
| Appendicular skeleton | The bones of the limbs plus the pectoral and pelvic girdles | Produces movement by anchoring muscles that move the arms and legs |
| Girdle | The bony ring (pectoral or pelvic) that connects a limb to the axial skeleton | The transfer point for forces between limbs and trunk |
| Skull | The 22 bones of the head: 8 cranial + 14 facial | Houses and protects the brain and the organs of sight, hearing, smell, and taste |
| Vertebral column | The 26 bones of the backbone: 24 vertebrae + sacrum + coccyx | Supports the head/trunk and protects the spinal cord |
| Thoracic cage | The sternum plus 12 pairs of ribs and their cartilages | Shields the heart and lungs; moves with breathing |
| Ossicles | The three tiny ear bones on each side (malleus, incus, stapes) | Transmit sound vibrations; counted with the axial skeleton |
| Hyoid bone | U-shaped bone in the anterior neck that supports the tongue | The only bone commonly taught to articulate with no other bone |
| Sesamoid bone | Small bone embedded in a tendon (e.g., patella) | Protects tendons from wear; count varies between people |
| Sutural (Wormian) bone | Small irregular bone that forms within a skull suture | Explains why skull bone counts vary person to person |

## How It Works / Step-by-Step Process

Classifying any bone into the right division is a four-step mental routine:

1. **Locate it.** Is the bone in the head, neck, or trunk (axis), or in a limb or girdle (appendage)?
2. **Check the midline.** Bones on or near the body's midline — skull, spine, ribs, sternum — are axial. Bones of the arms, legs, shoulders, and hips are appendicular.
3. **Name the subgroup.** If axial, is it skull, ossicle, hyoid, vertebral column, or thoracic cage? If appendicular, is it a girdle or a limb bone?
4. **State the job.** Protection/support for axial bones; movement/weight-bearing for appendicular bones.

## Example

A forensic anthropologist receives a box of bones labeled only "adult human, mixed." Sorting starts with the two-division test. The cranium, mandible, vertebrae, ribs, and sternum go in one tray: axial. The humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges, femur, tibia, fibula, patella, tarsals, metatarsals, clavicles, scapulae, and coxal bones go in the other: appendicular. Before naming a single bone, the anthropologist already knows the box should hold roughly 80 axial and 126 appendicular bones in an average adult — so a count of 205 or 207 is not an error, just a reminder that "206" is a typical figure, not a universal one.

## Common Confusions

| Do Not Confuse | With | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Skull | Cranium | Cranium = the 8 braincase bones; skull = cranium + 14 facial bones. "Skull" is the bigger set. |
| Clavicle | An axial bone | The clavicle touches the sternum but belongs to the pectoral girdle — appendicular. Touch ≠ membership. |
| 206 bones | A fixed number for every person | It is the typical adult count; sesamoid/sutural bones and fusion patterns cause variation. |
| Adult skeleton | Infant skeleton | Infants have more bones (commonly cited ~270) that fuse into the adult 206. |
| Sacrum | A separate bone | It is 5 fused vertebrae — part of the axial vertebral column. |
| Scapula | Axial bone | Scapula is pectoral girdle (appendicular). Test trap: "which is NOT axial — sternum, hyoid, scapula, sacrum?" → scapula. |
